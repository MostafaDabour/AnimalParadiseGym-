Starting at the gym is simpler than it looks. You do not need a complicated split, a supplement stack or two hours a day. You need a short list of basic movements, a schedule you can keep, and a way to get a little stronger each week.

This is the exact plan we give beginners at Animal Paradise Gyms in Vineland, NJ. It takes about 45 to 60 minutes, three times a week.

## Your weekly schedule

Train three days a week with at least one rest day between sessions. Alternate between Workout A and Workout B.

| Week | Monday | Wednesday | Friday |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Workout A | Workout B | Workout A |
| Week 2 | Workout B | Workout A | Workout B |
| Week 3 | Workout A | Workout B | Workout A |
| Week 4 | Workout B | Workout A | Workout B |

On the other days, walk for 20 to 30 minutes. Walking helps recovery, burns extra calories and makes the habit stick.

## The 5-minute warm-up (every session)

- 3 minutes on a bike, incline treadmill or stair climber at an easy pace
- 10 bodyweight squats
- 10 arm circles each way
- 1 light warm-up set of your first exercise at about half your working weight

## Workout A

| Exercise | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leg press | 3 | 10 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Chest press machine or dumbbell bench press | 3 | 8 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Lat pulldown | 3 | 10 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Dumbbell Romanian deadlift | 2 | 10 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Seated cable row | 2 | 10 to 12 | 60 sec |
| Plank | 3 | 20 to 30 sec | 45 sec |

## Workout B

| Exercise | Sets | Reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goblet squat | 3 | 8 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Shoulder press machine or seated dumbbell press | 3 | 8 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Chest-supported row or plate-loaded row | 3 | 10 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Hip thrust or glute bridge | 3 | 10 to 12 | 90 sec |
| Cable triceps pushdown | 2 | 12 to 15 | 60 sec |
| Dumbbell curl | 2 | 12 to 15 | 60 sec |

Machines are perfect for your first month. They guide the movement, so you can focus on control and feel the right muscles working. Once you are comfortable, you can swap in free-weight versions.

## How to pick the right weight

Choose a weight you can lift for the target reps with **2 or 3 reps left in the tank**. The last few reps should feel hard, but your form should look the same as the first rep.

- If you could easily do 5 more reps, go heavier next set.
- If your form breaks down before the target reps, go lighter.
- Write the weight down. Your notes are how you progress.

## How to progress over 4 weeks

This is called progressive overload, and it is the whole secret.

- **Week 1:** Learn the movements. Pick conservative weights and do 2 sets of everything.
- **Week 2:** Move to the full sets listed above. Aim for the top of the rep range.
- **Week 3:** When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, add the smallest weight jump available next time.
- **Week 4:** Keep adding a rep or a little weight where you can. Repeat the cycle for the next 4 weeks.

## Cardio for beginners

Lifting comes first, but cardio still matters for your heart and your conditioning. Walk on your rest days, or add 10 to 20 minutes of easy cardio after lifting. You do not need hard intervals in your first month.

## Eat and sleep for results

- **Protein:** include a protein source at every meal. A rough target is 0.7 to 1 gram per pound of your goal body weight.
- **Water:** sip during your session and drink through the day.
- **Sleep:** 7 to 9 hours. Muscle is built while you recover, not only while you train.

## Common beginner mistakes

1. **Doing too much too fast.** More sets do not mean more results in your first month. Soreness that lasts days means you overdid it.
2. **Changing the plan every week.** Stick with the same exercises for at least 4 weeks so you can see progress.
3. **Lifting with your ego.** Clean reps with a lighter weight build more muscle than sloppy heavy reps.
4. **Skipping the legs.** Your lower body has your biggest muscles. Train them every session.
5. **Comparing yourself to others.** Everyone at the gym started at day one.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days a week should a beginner go to the gym?
Three days a week is ideal for most beginners, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday. It gives your muscles time to recover between sessions and is easy to keep up long term. Add walking on your rest days.

### Should beginners do full body or split workouts?
Full-body workouts are best for beginners. You train every major muscle 3 times a week, which builds strength and skill faster than a body-part split. Splits make more sense after 6 to 12 months of consistent training.

### How long should a beginner workout be?
About 45 to 60 minutes, including a 5-minute warm-up. Longer sessions are not better when you are starting out.

### How heavy should a beginner lift?
Heavy enough that the last 2 or 3 reps of each set feel hard, while your form stays clean. If you could do 5 more reps, it is too light. If your form breaks down early, it is too heavy.

### Should beginners use machines or free weights?
Both work. Machines are easier to learn and safer while you build confidence, so they are a great start. Add free-weight versions like dumbbell presses and goblet squats as you get comfortable.

### How long until I see results from a beginner workout plan?
Most beginners feel stronger within 2 to 4 weeks and see visible changes around 8 to 12 weeks, as long as they train consistently, eat enough protein and sleep well.
