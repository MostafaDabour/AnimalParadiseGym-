If you want to know how to not gain weight over the holidays, the answer is less about willpower at Thanksgiving dinner and more about what you do on the other 30 or so days between now and New Year's. One big meal will not undo your progress. Six weeks of skipped workouts, nightly leftovers and extra drinks can.

You do not have to choose between enjoying the season and staying in shape. Here is a simple plan.

## How much weight do people really gain over the holidays?

Less than most people think, but it tends to stick. A well-known study run by the National Institutes of Health followed about 200 adults from fall into early spring. On average they gained just over a pound across that stretch, and most of it came between Thanksgiving and New Year's Day. Fewer than 10 percent gained more than 5 pounds.

The catch: they did not lose it afterward. When the volunteers were weighed again the next fall, they were still about a pound and a half heavier than the year before. A pound a year does not sound like much, but repeat that every holiday season and it adds up over a decade.

So the goal is not to be perfect. The goal is to come out of January close to where you went in.

## Keep training: your holiday workout plan

The single best thing you can do is not stop going to the gym. Shrink your workouts if December gets busy, but keep them on the calendar.

The CDC recommends that adults get at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening work on at least 2 days. That is your floor through the holidays, not your ceiling.

A holiday workout plan that works for busy weeks:

| Week type | Lifting | Cardio | Time per session |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal week | 3 to 4 full-body or split sessions | 2 to 3 walks or bike sessions | 45 to 60 min |
| Busy week | 2 full-body sessions | Daily 20 to 30 min walk | 30 to 40 min |
| Travel week | Bodyweight circuit 2 to 3 times | Walk every day | 20 to 30 min |

On short days, keep the big movements: a squat or leg press, a press, a row or pulldown, and a hinge like a Romanian deadlift. Two or three hard sets each is enough to hold on to strength and muscle. ## A Thanksgiving workout before the meal

A Thanksgiving workout will not "burn off" dinner, and that is not the point. It sets the tone for the day, helps your appetite feel more normal and keeps your streak alive. Try this full-body session in about 40 minutes:

1. 5 minutes of easy cardio to warm up
2. Goblet squat or leg press, 3 sets of 10
3. Dumbbell bench press or machine chest press, 3 sets of 10
4. Lat pulldown or cable row, 3 sets of 12
5. Dumbbell Romanian deadlift, 3 sets of 10
6. 10 minutes of incline walking or bike intervals

Then go enjoy your family. A walk after the meal is a great tradition, too.

## How to maintain weight during the holidays: eating

You do not need to diet in December. You need a few guardrails so the holidays stay a few special days instead of a six-week free-for-all.

### Eat normally on the days that are not holidays

The biggest mistake is turning one celebration into a whole week of eating. Have your Thanksgiving dinner, then go back to your usual breakfasts and lunches the next day. Pack leftovers into normal-sized portions or send some home with guests.

### Do not skip meals to "save up"

Showing up to dinner starving usually leads to eating fast and eating more. Have a normal breakfast with protein, and a light snack before the meal if it is late in the day.

### Build your plate in order

At the big meals, start with turkey or another protein and vegetables, then add the sides and dessert you really love. Protein is filling and helps you hold on to muscle. Our post on [protein shakes before or after a workout](protein-shake-before-or-after-workout.html) covers how to spread protein through your day.

### Pick your favorites, skip the rest

Is this a food you only get once a year? Grandma's pie, yes. Office cookies, maybe not. Enjoy what is worth it, without guilt.

### Watch the drinks

Alcohol adds up quickly. Each gram has about 7 calories, and festive drinks often come with sugar and cream on top of that. Drinking also tends to make late-night snacking more likely. If you drink, less is better. Alternate each drink with a glass of water and decide on a number before the party starts.

### Slow down

Put your fork down between bites and wait 10 or 15 minutes before going back for seconds.

## Check the scale, without stressing about it

Self-monitoring works. A 2018 randomized trial published in The BMJ followed 272 adults over the Christmas season in the UK. One group was asked to weigh themselves at least twice a week, ideally every day, and got practical weight-management tips plus a list showing how much activity it takes to burn off common holiday foods. For example, the list showed it takes about 21 minutes of running to burn off one mince pie. That group ended the study about half a kilogram (roughly 1 pound) lighter than the comparison group, which was not given those tools.

That is about the size of a typical holiday gain, so simple awareness went a long way.

How to use this:

- Weigh yourself first thing in the morning, after the bathroom, before eating.
- Watch the weekly trend. A salty dinner can add water weight overnight.
- If your trend is creeping up, tighten up for a few days. No crash diets needed.

## Sleep and stress matter more than you think

Late parties, travel and stress all cut into sleep, and when you are short on sleep, most people find hunger and cravings harder to manage. The CDC recommends at least 7 hours a night for adults. Protect your bedtime on normal nights, and do not schedule a 5AM workout after a midnight party just to prove a point. Recovery is part of the plan.

## Common holiday mistakes to avoid

- **The "I will start in January" mindset.** Taking six weeks off makes January much harder.
- **Trying to be perfect.** Strict rules usually lead to a big rebound. Aim for good enough.
- **Punishing workouts after a big meal.** Exercise is not a penalty for eating. Just get back to your normal routine.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much weight does the average person gain over the holidays?

Research from the National Institutes of Health found the average was around a pound between fall and early spring, with most of it gained between Thanksgiving and New Year's. Fewer than 10 percent of people gained more than 5 pounds. The bigger issue is that most people did not lose it afterward.

### Can you gain weight from one big meal?

Not real fat in any meaningful amount. The jump you see on the scale the next morning is mostly water, salt and food still in your system. It usually settles within a few days once you go back to normal eating.

### Should I work out on Thanksgiving?

If you enjoy it, yes. A morning workout is a nice way to start the day and keep your routine going. Think of it as a good habit, not a way to earn or burn off your dinner.

### How do I maintain weight during the holidays without dieting?

Keep training at least 2 times a week, eat normally on non-holiday days, start meals with protein and vegetables, limit alcohol and check your weight once or twice a week. These small habits are usually enough to come out of the season about where you started.

### What is a good holiday workout plan if I am busy?

Two full-body lifting sessions of 30 to 40 minutes plus a daily walk is enough to hold on to your progress. Focus on a squat or leg press, a press, a row and a hinge. When things calm down, go back to your usual plan.
