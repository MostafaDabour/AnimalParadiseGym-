Cardio Before or After Weights? What to Do for Your Goal

By the Animal Paradise coaching team · Vineland, NJ

Short answer: For most people, do weights first and cardio after. Lifting first lets you train hard while you are fresh, and research shows better lower-body strength gains with that order. Do cardio first only if endurance is your main goal, and keep a short 5 to 10 minute easy warm-up either way.

Cardio before or after weights is one of the most common questions we hear on the gym floor. The short version: if you care about getting stronger or building muscle, lift first and do your cardio after. If you are training for a race or endurance is the main goal, flip it.

Here is why, when to break the rule, and how to set up your week.

Why most people should do weights before cardio

Lifting is a skill. A good squat, deadlift or press needs focus, steady breathing and fresh legs. If you run hard for 30 minutes first, your legs and lungs are already tired when you get under the bar. You lift less weight, your form slips sooner, and the session gives you less of what you came for.

Research backs this up. A 2018 systematic review and meta-analysis in the Journal of Sports Sciences pooled studies that compared both orders in the same session. Lower-body strength improved more when strength training came before endurance training. The same review found that aerobic fitness (VO2max) improved about the same no matter which order people used.

So lifting first protects your strength, and your cardio fitness still improves.

Does cardio before lifting kill gains?

Not really, but it can make your lifting session worse. There is a difference between "cardio ruins muscle" and "being tired makes you lift less."

Larger reviews on concurrent training (doing cardio and strength in the same program) show that for most people, a sensible amount of cardio does not appear to stop muscle growth or gains in maximal strength. A 2021 review in Sports Medicine found the main thing that took a hit was explosive power, such as jumping and sprinting, and mostly when both were done in the same session. An older 2012 meta-analysis also found that running seemed to interfere with strength and size more than cycling did.

What that means for you:

  • A long, hard run right before leg day is the worst setup.
  • A short, easy bike or incline walk to warm up is not a problem.
  • Total amount matters. Two or three moderate cardio sessions a week is very different from daily hard runs.

We go deeper on how much cardio to do when bulking or cutting in Does Cardio Kill Gains?.

Cardio after lifting: how to do it well

Doing cardio after lifting is the setup we recommend most often. A few tips make it work better:

  • Keep it moderate. After a hard lifting session, 15 to 30 minutes of incline walking, cycling or the stair climber at a pace where you can still talk is plenty for most people.
  • Pick low-impact options after leg day. The bike or an incline walk is easier on tired legs than running.
  • Save hard intervals for another day if you are pushing your lifts. Sprints after a heavy squat session leave you sore and drained.
  • Eat afterward. If you are doing both in one visit, plan a meal or shake with protein when you are done. Our post on protein shakes before or after a workout covers timing.

When to do cardio first

There are good reasons to put cardio first:

  • Endurance is your main goal. If you are training for a 5K, a half marathon or a fitness test, your running or rowing quality matters most, so do it fresh.
  • It is a warm-up. Five to ten minutes of easy cardio before lifting raises your body temperature and gets you ready to move. That is not the "cardio first" people worry about.
  • It is upper-body day and the cardio is easy. A light bike ride before chest and arms will not affect your pressing much.
  • You will skip it otherwise. If you always leave the gym before your cardio, doing it first may be the only way it happens. Some cardio beats no cardio.

Cardio before or after weights: pick by goal

Your main goal Best order Notes
Build muscle Weights first Short, easy cardio after, or on separate days
Get stronger Weights first Keep hard cardio away from heavy leg days
Lose fat Weights first Lifting helps keep muscle while you diet; cardio adds calorie burn
Run a race or improve endurance Cardio first Lift after, or on separate days
General health Either Pick the order you will actually stick with
Brand new to the gym Weights first 5 to 10 minute easy warm-up, then your plan

Should I do cardio before or after lifting if I want to lose fat?

This is where people get stuck. It feels like cardio first should burn more fat. In practice, fat loss comes from your overall calorie balance over weeks, not from the order of one workout.

Lifting while you are losing weight is what helps you hold on to muscle, so you end up looking leaner and not just smaller. That is a strong reason to give your lifting your best energy and then add cardio after.

The best option: separate days or sessions

If you have the time, the cleanest answer is to not stack them at all. Doing cardio on different days, or a few hours apart from lifting, lets you train both with full effort.

Here is a simple week for someone lifting three days:

Day Training
Monday Full-body lifting, then 10 to 15 minutes easy bike
Tuesday 25 to 30 minute incline walk or easy cycling
Wednesday Full-body lifting
Thursday Rest or a relaxed walk
Friday Full-body lifting, then 10 to 15 minutes easy cardio
Saturday 30 minutes of cardio you enjoy, steady or light intervals
Sunday Rest

The CDC recommends adults get at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening work on at least 2 days. A week like this covers both without wrecking your lifting.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Long, hard cardio right before squats or deadlifts. This is the one order that clearly hurts your lifting session.
  • Skipping the warm-up because you think any cardio before weights is bad. A short easy warm-up helps.
  • Doing intervals every day. Hard cardio needs recovery too. Mix in easy sessions.
  • Ignoring food and sleep. Heavy training plus lots of cardio plus a big calorie deficit is a fast way to feel run down.
  • Stressing over order more than consistency. Showing up every week matters far more.

Quick answers

Is it better to do cardio before or after weights?

For most people, after. Lifting first lets you train with fresh muscles and good form, and research shows better lower-body strength gains with that order. Do cardio first only when endurance is your main goal.

Does cardio before lifting kill gains?

A short, easy warm-up does not. Long or hard cardio right before lifting can tire you out so you lift less, especially on leg day. Over time, a moderate amount of cardio does not appear to stop muscle growth for most people.

How long should I do cardio after lifting?

For most goals, 15 to 30 minutes at a moderate pace is enough. Use low-impact options like the bike or an incline walk after leg day. If you want longer or harder sessions, put them on separate days.

Should I do cardio first if I want to lose weight?

Usually no. Fat loss depends on your calorie balance over time, not the order of one workout. Lifting first helps you keep muscle while you lose fat, then cardio after adds extra calorie burn.

Can I do cardio and weights on the same day?

Yes. Lift first, then do moderate cardio, or split them into morning and evening sessions if you can. Keep very hard cardio away from your heaviest leg days.

Is a warm-up on the treadmill considered cardio before weights?

Not in the way people worry about. Five to ten minutes of easy cardio raises your body temperature and prepares you to lift. It is long, hard cardio before lifting that causes problems.
